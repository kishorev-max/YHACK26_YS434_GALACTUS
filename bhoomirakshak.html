The problem on the ground

Terrain that fails without notice

Meghalaya, Mizoram, Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim sit on steep, weathered slopes cut by roads and monsoon rainfall exceeding 3,000 mm a season. Slides often trigger with almost no visible precursor before the ground gives way.

The exact moment help is hardest to reach

The same storms that trigger slides also knock out cell towers and power in these districts. District-level bulletins arrive too late and too broad to act on, and there is usually no direct channel from a hazard reading to the police or fire post that would actually respond.

What makes this different

Runs without waiting for sensors

Deployable today on physically realistic simulated environmental data, then recalibrated as real gauges and soil probes come online — no multi-year data collection phase before it's useful.

Alerts survive the network failure

When towers go down, the alert doesn't stop — it hops over a LoRa mesh, village to village, until it reaches a connected relay that can reach the nearest police and fire station.

Village-level, not district-level

Micro-zonation scores individual settlements rather than whole districts, so a response team knows which specific hillside to act on first.

Risk × vulnerability, not just probability

Landslide probability is combined with what's actually nearby — homes, roads, a school — to produce an emergency priority order for responders with limited time and resources.

How BhoomiRakshak works, end to end

Six stages, from a simulated hillside to a decision a police station can act on.

1

Simulate rainfall, soil, slope, seismic and land-use data using realistic distributions and correlations for each site

2

Engineer features — moisture saturation, slope-rainfall interaction, deforestation exposure

3

Random Forest model outputs a landslide probability per site

4

Combine hazard probability with nearby population and infrastructure into a priority score

5

Route alert via cellular API, or LoRa mesh relay if towers are down

6

Dashboard shows the risk map and emergency priority list to the response team

Why Random Forest

Landslide triggers interact non-linearly — high rainfall only matters combined with steep slope and saturated soil — and the input features mix continuous readings (rainfall, moisture) with bounded indices (deforestation, road-cut proximity). A Random Forest handles that mix natively, resists overfitting on a modest simulated dataset, and exposes feature importances that make the model's reasoning explainable to a non-technical response team.

From prototype to field hardware

The dashboard you're using runs the same scoring logic that would sit on a Raspberry Pi or low-power microcontroller at each village node, paired with a LoRa radio for the mesh relay and a rain gauge / soil moisture probe once installed. Nothing here needs to be rebuilt for the field — the simulated inputs are replaced with sensor readings, and the model keeps working as it always did.
